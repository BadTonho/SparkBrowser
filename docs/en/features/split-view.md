# Split view

Drag a tab from the top bar or vertical sidebar into the first 32 logical pixels of either side of the page area. After 6 px of movement in any direction, a floating card with the tab title and site follows the pointer, including over native pages. A translucent preview marks the docking destination; release the mouse to confirm. Moving away removes the docking highlight while the card continues following the pointer. With a single tab, the card still appears, but no docking target is offered. Tools remains available as described below.

A new split opens at 50/50. The dragged tab takes the chosen side and becomes active. Its companion is the page active when dragging started; when dragging that active tab, the most recently used other tab from the same workspace is selected, with original tab order breaking ties. An existing split retains its ratio: a third tab replaces the chosen side while the old tab remains open. Dragging a paired tab to the opposite edge swaps the pair.

At either edge, the resident companion page immediately moves and resizes to its final pane while the dragged side keeps the translucent highlight. Other native pages are temporarily hidden without closing their documents. A hibernated companion, or one without a resident WebView, gets a neutral reserved pane and loads only after a successful drop. Leaving the edge restores the previous page arrangement while the card continues following the pointer.

The preview does not navigate, wake, hibernate, select or persist tabs. Dropping into the split restores any provisional tab reordering before saving the split, then applies the committed arrangement before removing the highlight; the companion does not expand between preview and confirmation. Dropping on the tab list reorders normally; dropping on a workspace button transfers the tab. Escape, pointer cancellation/lost capture, leaving the window, focus loss, opening a panel, or changing tabs/workspaces/layout cancels and restores geometry and the original order. Exiting split view remains available in Tools.

The native preview uses a transparent, mouse-ignoring owned window covering the main client area. The card stays inside the window, offset from the pointer, and shares this surface with the docking highlight. Geometry changes resize the surface; pointer updates only repaint its contents and are coalesced. The surface converts logical bounds to desktop physical coordinates using the main window's scale. Docking validates the original workspace, active tab, split identity and group order, then saves layout, selection and activation together before reconciling native documents. Failed persistence preserves the previous split, groups and resident history and displays an error.

In **Tools → Tabs**, select two different tabs from the current workspace and choose **Split selected tabs**. Pages appear side by side with an 8 px divider. Drag the divider or focus it and use Left/Right; Home restores 50%. Ratios range from 20% to 80%, with each native view at least 100 logical pixels wide.

## Independent pane tabs

In horizontal layout, each pane has its own tab list and **+** button aligned with the page divider, including while resizing. Both selected tabs are highlighted; the focused list has an accent underline. Lists scroll independently, including their new-tab buttons, and window controls retain their own space at the minimum 680 px window width. The address bar remains shared and follows the focused pane.

A new dock starts with the dragged tab alone on its chosen side; the companion receives the remaining workspace tabs in their original order. Tools-created splits and legacy sessions put all other tabs on the left. Selecting an existing tab changes only its owning pane. Each **+** creates and selects a tab in that list; Ctrl+T and reopened/received tabs without an explicit side use the destination workspace's focused group.

Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+Shift+Tab wrap within the focused group. Numbered selection and keyboard reordering also use that group; without split they use the workspace. Reordering changes only those tabs' slots in the global order. Dropping on the opposite tab list transfers and selects that tab; its source keeps its selected page or selects a replacement. Page-edge docking still swaps the two visible tabs when dragging one to the opposite edge, while docking a background tab keeps the replaced page as a background member.

Closing or moving a selected tab chooses the next tab in its group, or the previous tab at the end. An empty group ends the split; exiting split otherwise keeps the focused page and the global tab order. Vertical layout shows one combined list while preserving group membership and both selections. Returning to horizontal restores both lists.

Each workspace persists both groups, selections, ratio and focus across restart/recovery. SQLite migrations retain older splits, and backup version 4 stores/remaps group membership while versions 1–3 remain importable. Only the two selected pages are protected by split view against hibernation and bulk closing; background group tabs retain the ordinary memory policy.

Overlays hide both views and restore them afterward. Geometry updates reuse resident WebViews without navigating or changing focus; pointer movement is coalesced and the ratio persists when adjustment finishes. Windows uses native focus events; other engines use the generation-checked page focus bridge and require platform validation.

Docking presentation is separate from committed tab visibility and split layouts; unrelated session saves keep the original pair, selection and ratio. A resize recomputes both preview panes from current logical bounds. If the companion hibernates during the gesture, its reserved pane replaces the live content without waking it. Pointer-only updates repaint the card without repeating native geometry changes, and presentation focus events cannot select another tab. Divider ratio previews are also temporary: if saving fails, the divider and native pages return to the committed ratio. Replacing a pair persists the planned layout before closing resident pages, so a failed write preserves their documents and navigation history.

Local validation on November 2, 2026 used the consolidated checks and the native `tab-docking`, `persistence-split` and `workspace-switcher` scenarios on Windows. Docking covered live companion widths before release, confirmation without expansion, edge exit/cancellation, hibernated companions, stale requests, both layouts, resized bounds and physical/logical positioning at 100% scale, rollback/retry and restart. High-DPI/multi-monitor transitions and native behavior on other platforms remain unverified. See [native integration](../contributing/native-tests.md) to reproduce the isolated tests.
